# Practice Menu

A menu of small, widely studied practices for improving each life area. Offer it when the user wants ideas for their prototype plan, their weekly operating system, or a low score on the dashboard. The user picks; the menu never prescribes.

How to offer it:

- Suggest picking one to three practices, never more. Start with the easiest.
- Shrink any practice until starting feels easy. "Read 30 minutes" can become "read one page." A tiny start beats an impressive plan.
- Treat every pick as an experiment: it enters the prototype plan or weekly system with a review date, and the next review keeps or drops it based on what actually happened.
- These are plain summaries of common, well-studied ideas, not prescriptions. The user's own review evidence always wins over general advice.
- Anything involving diet, fasting, or exercise intensity deserves a doctor's opinion first, per `SAFETY_AND_LIMITS.md`.

## Physical and emotional well-being

1. Move for 30 minutes a day, at an effort that makes you breathe faster.
2. Protect 7 to 9 hours of sleep.
3. If your doctor agrees, try eating within an 8-hour window each day.
4. Drink 2 to 3 liters of water across the day.
5. Get 15 minutes of daylight, ideally in the morning.
6. Do strength training twice a week.
7. Get a full medical checkup once a year.
8. Eat fruits and vegetables in five colors each day.
9. Keep screens away for the last 90 minutes before bed.
10. Meditate 20 minutes a day, or start with two.

## Learning and creativity

1. Read 30 minutes a day; one page still counts.
2. Learn one new skill each year.
3. Learn one sentence of a foreign language each day.
4. Pick up a musical instrument.
5. Write regularly: notes, a journal, or something you publish.
6. Take one structured course instead of scattered videos.
7. Switch your phone to grayscale so it bores you.
8. Use your other hand for small tasks, or take an unfamiliar route.
9. Play strategy games such as chess.
10. When something goes wrong, practice the explanation that is specific and temporary, not permanent and personal.

## Work, career, and money

1. Save 20 percent of what you earn each month.
2. Keep one credit card, not five.
3. Learn the basics of personal finance.
4. Write a budget with goals you can name from memory.
5. End each work week with a short written summary and one honest reflection.
6. Put your goals where you see them every day; the fridge works.
7. Grow one small source of income beside your job.
8. Cover the big risks with insurance before optimizing anything else.
9. Review your career direction once a quarter.
10. Invest 5 percent of your income in yourself: courses, skills, and rooms full of people you can learn from.

## Relationships and community

1. Have a real conversation with three friends each week.
2. Eat dinner with your family three times a week.
3. Spend regular, undistracted time with the people closest to you.
4. Thank one person sincerely every day.
5. Volunteer once a month.
6. Practice listening without planning your reply.
7. Decline gatherings that give nothing and take a lot.
8. When angry, count to ten before you speak.
9. Smile before you answer the phone; it carries.

## Emotion and spirit

1. Keep a gratitude journal.
2. Keep one hobby that costs nothing.
3. Reserve two hours a week completely alone.
4. Spend more time near people who lift your energy.
5. Take one day a week lighter on screens.
6. Walk in a park; green places do quiet work.
7. When you decide to do something, start within five seconds.
8. Tidy one small space for ten minutes a day.
9. Practice slow, deep breathing when the day tightens.
